## Fact sources (checked)
- 1 001 PS, four turbochargers, 0–100 km/h in 2.5 s, top speed 407 km/h: Bugatti press release *“Bugatti Veyron – Legends: the modern-day hyper sports car”* (2019), newsroom.bugatti.com
- Top-speed run at Ehra-Lessien, 2005 (TÜV-verified average 408.47 km/h; official figure 407 km/h): Bugatti newsroom *“407 km/h – A Milestone in Automotive History”*
- Super Sport, 1 200 PS, 431.072 km/h Guinness production-car record, 2010, Ehra-Lessien, driver Pierre-Henri Raphanel: Bugatti press release *“Landspeed world record with the Bugatti Veyron 16.4 Super Sport”* (2010); Autocar; Motor1
- 450 cars in total (300 coupés + 150 Grand Sport / Grand Sport Vitesse roadsters); last car “La Finale” shown at the Geneva Motor Show, March 2015: Bugatti *“Bugatti sells the last Veyron”* (2015); paultan.org; evo
- Named after Pierre Veyron, Bugatti test driver and engineer who won the 1939 24 Hours of Le Mans with Jean-Pierre Wimille: Bugatti *“80 years ago: Bugatti wins 24 Hours of Le Mans”*; *“Bugatti Veyron – the legacy behind a legendary name”*
- Ten radiators: Bugatti / Robb Report Bugatti facts
- Volkswagen Group acquired the Bugatti brand in 1998; production at Molsheim (Alsace) from 2005: Bugatti newsroom *“15 years of Bugatti Veyron – how it all began”*; *“The Bugatti workshop in Molsheim opens”*

Deliberately left out because sources disagree: the “fuel tank empties in 12 minutes” claim, and the exact model of Pierre Veyron's 1939 Le Mans car (Type 57C vs 57S Tank).
